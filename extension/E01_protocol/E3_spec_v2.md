## Source, decision time and panel

Primary series is official Cboe VIX30D daily close; candidate secondary series are VIX9D, VIX3M and VIX6M from official Cboe daily closes. At E3 start identify the official source URLs, published methodology/vintage notes and download date, and freeze the exact retrieved files and their last common observed date before calculating losses. The cutoff is the maximum actually observed valid VIX30D close date in that frozen snapshot, no later than retrieval date; record the literal date and hash in E3 input manifest before any scoring. Secondary series end at their own last observed valid date no later than primary cutoff. If source or history is unavailable, retain a named unestimable test with reason. Daily rows must have unique increasing exchange dates and positive finite closes. Duplicate, missing or nonpositive rows are excluded and enumerated; a missing trading session does not become an invented observation. Inner joins across the maturity and VIX30D feature dates are recorded. Retrospective official files may embody later revisions; this is a historical backtest, not a proven real-time-vintage experiment.

Origin t is immediately after day-t official close becomes available. Let x_t=log(VIX_t) for the target maturity. Horizons h={5,10,20} observed sessions. Direct target y_(t,h)=h^(-1) sum_{j=1}^h x_(t+j). Build each target only if all h subsequent observed trading sessions have valid closes; exclude incomplete tail, gaps and missing values with reason. Predict the average future log level; score squared error in that same unit. No recursive curve forecast, future peak or future-onset knowledge.

HAR regressors are intercept, x_t, mean(x_t..x_(t-4)), mean(x_t..x_(t-21)). Require all 22 immediately preceding observed exchange sessions valid; no skipping invalid closes inside a lag window. HAR-X adds exactly five features from VIX30D: active, shock size, age, decay and active×five-session log change. Both models use the identical eligible origin/target rows for each maturity and horizon. For secondary maturities use their own x, HAR lags and targets but the same VIX30D shock state on the joined decision date.

## Causal VIX30D shock-state machine

Traverse sorted valid VIX30D exchange sessions once. At session t, a candidate onset occurs if V_t−V_(t−1)>=7 VIX points or V_t−V_(t−5)>=10 points, requiring those exact prior valid exchange-session closes and no intervening invalid row. Accept only if more than 30 calendar days since the last *accepted* onset; otherwise ignore it. There is no retrospective merge. At acceptance, replace prior active shock, set age=0 and running maximum=V_t. The baseline is the arithmetic mean of exactly the 20 immediately preceding valid VIX30D closes; if unavailable, record onset but feature state is ineligible until the next accepted onset with baseline. At each subsequent observed valid session increment age by one and update running maximum through t. Active=1 at age 0..119, otherwise 0. With valid active state, size=log(running_max/baseline), age=integer session age, decay=exp(-age/tau), and fifth feature=active×(log V_t−log V_(t−5)), requiring exact five-session history. Inactive size, age, decay and fifth feature are zero; active indicator zero. If active but required history is missing, mark entire HAR-X row ineligible and remove its matched HAR row. No clipping of negative shock size. Missing VIX observations break feature-lag eligibility and are never forward-filled. Every shock feature at forecast date t is computed solely from observations dated <=t. No feature may use a curve fitted to a completed shock, the shock's eventual peak, or its end date. The running maximum is through t only, the baseline precedes the accepted onset, and five-session change ends at t. State transitions depend only on information available through t; monthly refits do not reset state.

## Monthly fits and training-only tau selection

For VIX30D, historical estimation starts with available 1990–1999 data and first OOS origin is first available session in January 2000; if that training window is unavailable, primary is unestimable rather than shortened. For each secondary maturity, first OOS origin is first available session after ten complete calendar years of its valid history and at least three annual inner validation years. Refit before the first decision session of every calendar month, using records available by previous month end C. A training origin qualifies only if its full target endpoint date <=C; features use dates <=origin. Freeze coefficient vector and tau for the month; update daily features causally. At each h, estimate separate OLS intercept models by deterministic SVD least squares on identical complete rows. Require full column rank at tolerance max(n,p)*eps*largest singular value and n>=p+1; otherwise month/h/maturity is unestimable with both forecasts missing. No standardization outside the training fold.

HAR-X tau grid is {5,10,20,40,80,120} sessions. At each monthly refit, choose it using the last three *complete calendar years* ending before C; for each of those three annual validation folds, fit on all earlier origins whose target endpoints are before that fold's January 1, then predict eligible origins in the fold whose target endpoints are <=that fold's December 31 and <=C. Require all three folds estimable and at least one scored origin each; otherwise the monthly HAR-X fit is unestimable. Pool fold squared errors by total origin count, choose minimum aggregate mean squared error, exact tie -> smaller tau. Then fit both final models on all matured training origins through C. Annual validation uses no test-month outcomes. Store each fold's train/validation dates, row counts, tau losses and coefficient hashes, final coefficients, cutoff, eligible training endpoint and selected tau. At later months rerun selection on that month's training history only. This deterministic rule is fixed before loss access.

## Inference and displays

At each paired scored origin define d_t=(y_t−HAR_t)^2−(y_t−HARX_t)^2; positive favors HAR-X. Primary test: VIX30D h=10 on active origins. Report paired n, calendar dates, number of accepted active episodes, both MSEs, mean d, and relative gain mean(d)/HAR MSE (undefined if denominator zero). Two-sided alpha=.05; an improvement claim requires mean d>0 and raw primary p<.05. Report the primary regardless of sign.

The fixed secondary family has 23 tests: VIX30D h10 all days (1); VIX30D h5 and h20 active and all days (4); each of VIX9D,VIX3M,VIX6M at h5,h10,h20 active and all days (18). Every planned slot remains in the Holm family; unestimable slots receive p=1 for adjustment and an explicit reason, with no inferential effect claim. Holm adjusted p: sort raw p ascending with stable declared slot order; adjusted p_(i)=min[1,max_{j<=i}((23−j+1)p_(j))]. Report raw and adjusted values and paired counts. Onset+5 display is descriptive only: for each accepted onset with an eligible origin at the fifth subsequent observed session, report paired losses and d without p-values.

For a test, order the full eligible paired-origin daily grid t=1..T by exchange session date, including inactive days for an active test. For all-days use m=mean(d), scores g_t=d_t−m. For active use I_t, A=sum I_t, p=A/T, m=sum I_t d_t/A, and g_t=I_t(d_t−m)/p. The latter retains zero scores on inactive dates and calendar-session lag spacing; never compress active dates. Define gamma_l=T^(-1)sum_{t=l+1}^T g_t g_(t−l), Bartlett long-run V=gamma_0+2sum_{l=1}^{h−1}(1−l/h)gamma_l and variance of m as V/T. Harvey factor F=sqrt[(T+1−2h+h(h−1)/T)/T]; statistic=F*m/sqrt(V/T), two-sided p from Student t with T−1 degrees of freedom. This fixes the Newey–West lag h−1, divisor T, no automatic lag tuning, and the specified Harvey convention. Require T>=max(h+2,3), for active A>=max(3,h), at least two accepted active episodes, positive finite V and positive finite F argument; otherwise report unestimable. Active episode count uses distinct accepted onsets governing active scored dates. Preserve T, A, V, F, statistic and exclusions for independent recomputation. Nested models, overlapping targets, persistence and sparse active days limit textbook DM calibration; no causal or trading-value claim follows from p alone.

## Crisis units, bootstrap and sensitivity

For each maturity, construct crisis units from accepted VIX30D onsets known by their occurrence dates, using their prospective active intervals [onset session, onset+119th valid observed VIX30D session]. Merge intervals that overlap or touch on the valid VIX30D session index, taking transitive closure. Use the same frozen crisis ID across horizons and active/all-day subsets. Assign each paired scored origin in a merged interval to exactly one crisis; show accepted-onset IDs, interval bounds, paired-origin counts and the number K of crisis units represented in each of the 24 test sets (primary and 23 secondary). A scored active origin without a valid accepted-onset assignment is a construction failure. For all-day tests, partition scored inactive runs outside crisis intervals into consecutive nonoverlapping h-session blocks, last short block retained; report these separately from K. Exclusions and missing dates are recorded before unit construction; do not bridge a missing paired origin as if it were scored.

For each active test, bootstrap whole represented crisis units, never individual dates. For each all-day test, resample crisis units and inactive h-session blocks independently within their two strata, preserving the observed count of each type. Use 1,999 draws, PCG64 NumPy seed from `protocol_core.seed('Extension','E3','crisis_bootstrap',maturity,h,subset)` with base SEED=5902026; save generator version and replicate IDs. In each draw sample K crisis IDs with replacement (and the observed number of inactive blocks for all-day tests), concatenate all paired d values in selected blocks and compute the observation-weighted mean d. The point statistic is observed paired mean d. Use the empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 1,999 bootstrap means with NumPy linear interpolation as the two-sided 95% interval; report both limits and fraction of draws <=0 as a diagnostic. No positive robustness finding unless the lower interval limit >0. Require K>=10 represented crisis units and finite paired d for inferential bootstrap; if fewer, mark bootstrap inconclusive and any positive claim unavailable, while retaining descriptive point results. For all-day tests also require at least one inactive block if inactive scored origins exist; a missing stratum is an implementation failure. Zero valid draws or a nonfinite interval is unestimable.

For each test, leave out one of those same K crisis units at a time from the scored paired origins, keeping the previously saved monthly model forecasts and tau fixed: this asks whether one test crisis drives the realized gain, without retraining a model on a different historical information set. For all-day tests keep all inactive blocks. Recompute paired n, remaining K, mean d and relative gain for every omitted crisis; identify the omitted onset interval. Do not replace a missing forecast or refit. If remaining n=0, HAR MSE=0 for relative gain, or values are nonfinite, display `unavailable` with reason; mean d may still be displayed when only the relative denominator is zero. Stability requires every computable leave-one-crisis-out mean d>0 and every required omission computable. Require K>=10 for the stability conclusion; otherwise report descriptive omissions only.

A primary headline positive result requires simultaneously mean d>0 and the daily two-sided DM/HAC/Harvey raw p<.05, crisis bootstrap lower 95% limit >0 with K>=10, and all K leave-one-crisis-out mean differences >0 with every omission computable. If any component is unavailable or inconclusive, report the primary result as inconclusive for a positive claim. Negative and inconclusive estimates remain reportable. A secondary positive claim additionally requires its Holm-adjusted daily p<.05 and the same bootstrap and leave-one-crisis-out conditions; a missing secondary slot retains p=1 in the fixed Holm family and cannot support a positive claim. Daily DM with Newey–West lag h-1 remains the prespecified overlapping-horizon test; the cluster checks are additional dependence safeguards.
